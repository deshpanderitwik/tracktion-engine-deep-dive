08 · audio files & time-stretch

Audio files & time-stretch

How sample data moves from disk into a playing graph. AudioFile is the identity; AudioFileCache is the shared pool of readers; AudioFileManager owns the whole subsystem. Behind them sit a format stack, a proxy generator, and a pluggable time-stretch library.

Concept: file, cache, reader, stretcher

Every audio clip in a Tracktion Edit points at a file. But a pointing-at is not a reading-from. The engine separates the identity of a file (AudioFile — a hashable wrapper around a juce::File plus its AudioFileInfo) from the act of reading it, which is done through a shared AudioFileCache. When a WaveNode needs samples at playback time it does not open the file; it asks the cache for a reader.

The cache returns Reader handles backed by BufferedFileReader / BufferedAudioReader that stream ahead on a background thread and hand out blocks via a lock-free AudioFifo. If the reader isn't ready yet (seek, cold cache), a FallbackReader hands back silence so the audio callback never blocks. Behind the cache sits the AudioFileManager, an engine-owned object that tracks open files, invalidates entries when they change on disk, and manages the thumbnail cache.

Implementation: layers, top to bottom

At the top: AudioFile and AudioFileInfo (sample rate, length, channels, metadata). Immutable value types. Equality is file-path-based. This is what clips store.

Under that: AudioFileManager, which extends juce::AsyncUpdater, and its partner AudioFileCache. Together they own the pool of live readers. The manager hands out LoopInfo (beat-markers and BPM for loopable files), AudioProxyGenerator tasks (generate down-sampled proxies for slow-disk files on a background thread), and the RecordingThumbnailManager / SmartThumbnail pair that drive the waveform UI.

Under that: the format layer. AudioFormatManager wraps JUCE's built-in formats (WAV, AIFF, FLAC, Ogg) and adds Tracktion-specific ones: FFmpegEncoderAudioFormat (MP4/AAC export via ffmpeg), FloatAudioFileFormat (the engine's own lossless intermediate), LAMEManager (MP3 export via LAME — also its own class because LAME is loaded dynamically), RexFileFormat (Propellerhead REX slices), MemoryMappedFileReader for mmap-backed fast access.

To the side: the time-stretch subsystem, which lives in its own directory (tracktion_engine/timestretch/) rather than under audio_files/. TimeStretcher is the facade; it dispatches to one of several licensed libraries (Elastique, RubberBand, SoundTouch) controlled by compile-time flags (TRACKTION_ENABLE_TIMESTRETCH_*). ReadAheadTimeStretcher is the read-ahead wrapper that TimeStretchingWaveNode uses at playback — this is the one CreateNodeParams::readAheadTimeStretchNodes from diagram 4 refers to, still flagged "TEMPORARY" in the source. BeatDetect and TempoDetect are the analysis side: detecting tempo and beat markers when a file is imported.

Tradeoff: readers are a pool, not per-clip

A naive design would give each WaveNode its own AudioFormatReader. Sharing through a cache trades simplicity for memory and disk-handle savings: 20 clips of the same loop open one reader, not 20. The cost is synchronisation — the cache has to manage contention for its read-ahead buffers across workers. And the "reader isn't ready" fallback path exists because the cache cannot always have fresh samples at the moment an audio block needs them. Getting that failure mode to return silence instead of blocking is what keeps a seek from glitching the audio thread.

Time-stretch pluralism is the other big tradeoff. Three libraries with different quality/CPU/license profiles are supported through a common Mode enum. Embedders pick which to ship via compile flags. This is good for users (choose your licence story) and inconvenient for reading — whether a given build even has time-stretch at all depends on macros.

Open questions

The proxy-generator path (AudioProxyGenerator) predates modern SSDs; for an NVMe-backed session it is likely wasted work. Does the engine have a way to opt out cleanly, or does it always generate proxies? Worth checking when writing diagram 10 (recording), since that's where proxies interact with freeze.

Notable omission: the diagram does not cover the editor-side waveform caches in detail. SmartThumbnail and RecordingThumbnailManager are named but their lifecycle belongs in a UI-centric doc, not the engine core.
